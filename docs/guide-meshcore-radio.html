MCMeshCore guide

Choosing a MeshCore radio

You only need one radio to get on MeshCore. These are the ones our regulars recommend, and how to get MeshCore onto them.

Radios we recommend

Heltec T096

Best value

The best price-to-performance ratio and a good all-around unit. Comes fully assembled with a case, 3000 mAh battery, GPS and antenna.

View on Amazon →

SenseCAP MeshTracker X1

Card-sized

The best credit-card-style radio. Dual-band GPS, IP66 rated, LR2021 LoRa chip. Often backordered.

View at Seeed Studio →

Both are sold as “Meshtastic” devices. That’s fine: the same hardware runs MeshCore once you flash it.

T096: keep the screen attached

The T096’s BLE and GPS antennas are built into the screen bezel. If you disconnect the screen ribbon cable, keep that hardware attached so Bluetooth still works when you need it, for OTA updates and such.

Flashing MeshCore

The easiest way to flash MeshCore is the Mesh America Device Configurator. It runs in your browser.

Two alternative firmwares are popular here:

FirmwareWhy use it

Easy SkyMesh PowerSavingMuch better battery life on ESP32 and nRF52 boards. Has its own web flasher.

Antennas and power

Radio power and antenna gain are two different numbers. Power is in dBm, antenna gain is in dBi, and the FCC limit covers both together.

On this band the FCC caps the radio at 1 W (30 dBm) with an antenna of 6 dBi or less. That works out to a ceiling of about 36 dBm for radio power plus antenna gain.

Radio powerLargest legal antennaExamples
30 dBm6 dBi1 W radios
28 dBmAbout 8 dBiT096, Tracker V2

Coax loss counts in your favor, so you can go a little higher if you actually measure your loss.

Higher gain isn’t always better

Higher-gain antennas squash the signal toward the horizon. Somewhere around 5-6 dBi on a ridge still reaches down into the valleys, and 3-5 dBi suits a house in a hollow aiming up at the ridge.
